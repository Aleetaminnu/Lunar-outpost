# Teacher guide: Outpost Zero (one page)

**Ages:** 7-12. **Time:** 20-30 minutes. **Needs:** a phone, tablet or computer with a browser. No account or download.

## Learning goals
Students experience that real missions have limited power, so life support, radiation shielding, food and science compete. They see that space weather and radio delay change decisions.

## Before playing (3 minutes)
Ask: "If you had only 10 batteries for a whole day on the Moon, what would you spend them on?" Write answers on the board.

## Playing (15-20 minutes)
- Pairs work well: one person moves, one reads the messages.
- Start on Normal. Younger students can use Easy.
- Tell them to read the yellow line (what the nearby building does) and the green and red numbers that appear after each action.

## Discussion questions
1. What did you spend power on first each day? Why?
2. What happened when you skipped the shield? What does a real crew do about radiation?
3. On Mars, Earth answered a day later. How would that change how a crew works?
4. Why does the Moon need a stronger shield than Mars in this game? (Moon surface has about twice the radiation.)

## Extensions
Compare the Moon and Mars stages. Chart each team's power use between survival and science. Look up the real solar flare data on NASA's DONKI site.

## Notes
Numbers like power and food are simplified for the game. Radiation levels and space events are based on real NASA and partner data, listed in `docs/SOURCES.md`.
